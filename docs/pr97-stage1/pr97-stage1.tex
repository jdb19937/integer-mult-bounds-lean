\title[A cover-free bit supplier]{A cover-free bit supplier for integer multiplication below
$n \log n$ in Lean}

\author{Dan Brown$^*$}
\email{jdb1729@gmail.com}
\thanks{$^*$Author of \emph{The Da Vinci Code}.}
\author{Kenny Daniel}
\email{platypii@gmail.com}

\date{October 2026}

\begin{abstract}
The Lean development~\cite{IMB} proves that one fixed machine of Mathlib's \lean{Turing.FinTM2}
model multiplies $n$-bit numbers in time $O\bigl(n(\log n)^{1-\kappa}\bigr)$. With the T16
complex supplier in place, its bit interchange, the Shared50 network of~\cite{OAI} with
exponent $\tau = 1 - 296\cdot 10^{-11}$, bounds the saving at $\kappa = 88279 \cdot 2^{-46}
\approx 2^{-29.57}$. We replace that interchange with the two-stage partial-swap word of
PR97~\cite{PR97}, built on Swapnil Jain's round-7 witness~\cite{SJ}. The word needs no routes,
charts, cover classes or helper hosting, so it runs on the existing recursive interchange
machine. The proof adds a constructive exchange lemma, which makes a rank-$r$ frame change $r$
child calls between lower-triangular passes; an explicit height bound, which fixes the machine's
prime; a machine generic in the bit network; and a seam that selects the supplier. The new
saving is $\kappa = 111364923 \cdot 2^{-46} \approx 2^{-19.27}$. We also record why the selected
PR589 supplier does not port, rank the cover-free suppliers of~\cite{CS}, and describe the next
stage, the opposite-bank compiler of~\cite{ICE}, at about $2^{-14.5}$.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The saving $\kappa$ of~\cite{IMB} is the least of five margins of the compact multiplier, with
$\varepsilon = 31/64$ and chunk exponent $c = 7/8$:
\[
  \varepsilon c (1-\tau),\quad \varepsilon(1-\lambda'),\quad (1-\varepsilon)(1-\tau),\quad
  \tfrac12 - \delta - \varepsilon(1+2^{-33}),\quad \varepsilon(1-c).
\]
Here $\tau$ is the exponent of the bit interchange, $\lambda < \lambda' < 1$ are the layer
exponents, and the constraints $\tau < \lambda$ and $\sigma < \lambda$ tie the layers to both
suppliers. Kenny Daniel's T16 node on the layer machine gives $\sigma < 999/1000$
(\lean{br\_branching\_999}). With T16 the binding margin is the layout margin
$\varepsilon c (1-\tau)$, so the bit supplier sets $\kappa$: at Shared50's $1 - \tau = 2.96 \cdot
10^{-9}$ this is $88279 \cdot 2^{-46}$.

Each claim carries a tag. \tagL{} marks a theorem checked in~\cite{IMB} under the name given, on
the axioms \lean{propext}, \lean{Classical.choice} and \lean{Quot.sound}. \tagP{} marks a
written argument. \tagC{} marks a number computed by a script from pinned upstream data.

\section{The machine model}\label{sec:model}

A node of the recursive interchange machine holds records addressed by two fields $H$ and $D$ of
$m$ sub-fields each, and interchanges them. For an idempotent $U$ write
\[
  D_U(h, d) = \bigl((1-U)h + Ud,\; Uh + (1-U)d\bigr),
\]
so the parent interchange is $D_1$. The node's cheap operations are the field operations of
\lean{AffineFieldProgram.Op}: scalings, ordered shifts, the cross shears $D \mathrel{+}= KH$, and
XOR gates between role arrays. On the address coordinates of one role these act by lower
triangular matrices in the $H$-before-$D$ order. A child is \lean{interchange i j}, which swaps
$H_i$ with $D_j$ for independent $i$ and $j$, on volume $V/W$ for $W$ roles. A node with $s$
children over $W$ roles costs $O(V)$ plus its children, and the uniform recursion gives
$O\bigl(V w^{\tau}\bigr)$ for every $\tau$ with $s/W < m^{\tau}$.

\section{Choosing a bit supplier}\label{sec:choice}

\subsection{The Shared50 family}
\tagC{} Shared50 has $h = 50$, $m = h^3$, and deficit $\eta = D/(Wm) = 23/661055000$. With
rank-one children its saving is $\eta/\ln m = 2.96 \cdot 10^{-9}$. In the three-stage cube
family $\eta = (v - 6h^2)/(2(v+R+h)h^3)$ with $v = \binom{h}{3}$; more roles $R$ only lower it,
and the loss $6h^2$ forces $h > 36$. The family is capped at $7.99 \cdot 10^{-8}$ with rank-one
children. A better shear program gains nothing, because lower-triangular factors preserve the
rank of the $H$-to-$D$ block, so \lean{exists\_prime\_shear}'s $\operatorname{rank} A$ children
are already minimal.

\subsection{PR589}
The selected bit supplier of~\cite{CS}, PR589, has $a_b = 10/5769$. Our earlier
note~\cite{HOLE} shows that its accounting assumes free port routes, which this machine has to
pay for. A further study \tagP\tagC{} sharpens that verdict:
\begin{itemize}
\item In upstream's geometry, $970$ of the word's $5472$ gates join registers of two different
  ports, so every column needs common coordinates at every invocation.
\item With split windows the route question reduces to one scalar condition per port. One
  window family serves $88$ of the $111$ E8 ports with no route cost, with full-size solves to
  residuals below $10^{-8}$.
\item Hosting the full word over those $88$ ports costs about $0.66$ rank units per role, so the
  moment at $\lambda = 1$ is $1.0073$ and nothing is saved. Keeping $97$ ports with corrections
  gives $a_b \le 4.06 \cdot 10^{-4}$ only if a corner defect can be repaired with two rank-one
  children per family, the parity minimum; no such repair is known.
\item A word whose ports all share one line saves nothing in any geometry.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Cover-free suppliers}
\tagC{} Every supplier of~\cite{CS} with $a_b > 1.24 \cdot 10^{-4}$ needs a class-indexed cover
or the compressed nine-stage geometry with port routes. Those that run on this machine:

\section{Frame changes on the machine}\label{sec:exchange}

\begin{theorem}[\lean{M8.exchange\_factor}, \lean{M8.ExchangeProgram.program\_run},
\lean{program\_interchanges}; \tagL]\label{thm:exchange}
Let $U = E_1 \Pi E_2$ be an idempotent over a commutative ring, with $E_1, E_2$ invertible lower
triangular and $\Pi$ a pivot matrix with pivot pairs $L$. Put $M = E_2 E_1$, $P_E = \Pi\Pi^{\mathsf
T}$, $P_F = \Pi^{\mathsf T}\Pi$ and
\[
  K = M P_E - P_F M + P_F M P_E - \Pi^{\mathsf T}.
\]
Then $D_U$ equals: transform $H$ by $E_1^{-1}$ and $D$ by $E_2$; shear $D \mathrel{+}= KH$; swap
$H_i$ with $D_j$ for each $(i, j) \in L$; shear $D \mathrel{-}= KH$; transform back. It uses
exactly $|L| = \operatorname{rank} U$ interchanges.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Sketch]
In the coordinates $(E_1^{-1}h, E_2 d)$ the map is $g = 1 + CR$ with $C = [\Pi; -M\Pi]$ and
$R = [-M, 1]$. Idempotency of $U$ is $\Pi M \Pi = \Pi$. The matrix $K$ satisfies $KE = ME - F$
and $F^{\mathsf T}K = E^{\mathsf T} - F^{\mathsf T}M$ for the pivot columns $\Pi = EF^{\mathsf
T}$, so conjugating by the shear $S = \bigl[\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\K&1\end{smallmatrix}\bigr]$
gives $SgS^{-1} = 1 + [E; -F][-E^{\mathsf T}, F^{\mathsf T}]$, the swap on the pivot pairs.
\end{proof}

For nested frames $A \le B$ the frame change is $D_A D_B = D_{B-A}$ (\lean{exchange\_comp}), of
rank $\operatorname{rank} B - \operatorname{rank} A$ (\lean{rank\_sub\_add}) \tagL. With rank-one
children the cost along a wire telescopes to its final rank minus its initial rank, so the count
$s$ does not depend on the intermediate frames \tagP.

\subsection{The prime}
The machine runs over $\Z/q^b$ for one odd prime $q$. Lower-triangular factorizations are not
unique, so a factorization chosen by \lean{Classical.choice} has no height. The development
proves a height-bounded elimination (\lean{M8.Height.exists\_bounded}) \tagL: for an $n \times n$
rational matrix whose entries have numerator and denominator at most $H$, some factorization has
all entries below an explicit $G(n, H)$. Every number the exchange program needs is then below
\lean{coefficientBound} $= 2^{e}$, $e$ a sealed choice above $2^{2^{16}}$, for $n \le 529$ and $H
= 10^{10000}$ (\lean{numbers\_le\_coefficientBound}) \tagL. The kernel never evaluates the
tower.

Two edits to the Shared50 network files make that prime available. \lean{Shared50ModularControl.prime}
is chosen beyond \lean{coefficientBound} and Shared50's denominators
(\lean{coefficientBound\_lt\_prime}), and the coefficient classes \lean{Occurs} and
\lean{ScaleOccurs} admit every rational within the bound \tagL.

\section{The PR97 word}\label{sec:word}

\tagC{} The word has $h = 23$, $v = \binom{23}{3} = 1771$, $m = h^2 = 529$, $N = v^2 = 3136441$,
$R = 28866$ side roles per invocation, $W = 2N + 2vR = 108516254$ roles, and rank mass
\[
  s = Wm - N + 2vh(h-1) = 57403754177.
\]
The pinned ledger replays: $1080616$ events, $s$ as stated, and the complete forward and
reflected $\F_2$ shear.

\subsection{Plain frames}
The development uses the simplest nested frames the gates allow \tagP\tagC. Readouts start at
frame $0$; the source of triple $S$ sits at the line $t_S$; node $n$ of the side DAG sits at
$\operatorname{span}(n) = \sum_{S \in \operatorname{supp} n} \Q t_S$; the roots read at the
hyperplanes $t_T^{\perp}$ and the star spaces $U_c$. The frames are the $G$-orthogonal
projectors for $G = I - J/9$ on $\Q^{23}$ (\lean{M8.Frames.Projector}). Nondegeneracy is a
theorem: every support lies in a star, and on $U_c$ the form $G$ is the Euclidean form of the
pair vectors, which is positive definite. The plain word has the same $s$ as upstream's
design-B frames. Every projector entry has numerator and denominator at most $10^{200}$
(\lean{frame\_height\_le}) \tagL.

\subsection{The local word}
The local word is $\mathrm{OPS}' \,;\, \mathrm{RR} \,;\, \mathrm{OPS}'^{-1} \,;\, \mathrm{OPS}
\,;\, \mathrm{RR} \,;\, \mathrm{OPS}^{-1}$, with $\mathrm{OPS}$ the compiled XOR list of the side
DAG and $\mathrm{RR}$ the root reads \tagL:
\begin{itemize}
\item \lean{compile\_correct}: for every valid DAG, from zero side slots, each root's slot holds
  the root's value.
\item \lean{word\_run}: on arbitrary contents the garbage of the side slots cancels over $\F_2$.
\item The certificate data, as Lean structures in sixteen chunks, pass \lean{decide +kernel} in
  about $2.5$ minutes; they give \lean{dag\_valid} and \lean{supp\_add}.
\item \lean{word\_run\_target}: by the triple identity
  $\sum_{c \in T}[c \in S,\ S \cap T = \{c\}] + \sum_{c \in T}[c \in S] \equiv [S = T] \pmod 2$,
  the word adds $x_t$ into $y_t$ for every triple and leaves everything else unchanged.
  \lean{reflected\_run\_source} is the bank-exchanged word.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{The global node}
Stage one runs the doubled local word in invocation $T$ with frames $K \otimes Q_T$; stage two
runs it reversed and complemented, lifted to $(1 - P_S) \otimes 1 + P_S \otimes K$, with the banks
exchanged. The data role $X_{ST}$ starts in $P_S \otimes Q_T$, and after the rank-one correction
$Y_{ST}$ ends in $1 - P_S \otimes Q_T$. Since $D_U D_{1-U} = D_1$, every contract role ends with
its route's stream, address fields exchanged \tagL:
\begin{itemize}
\item \lean{node\_scalar}: on scalars the node maps $(x, y) \mapsto (y, x)$;
\item \lean{node\_run}: the identity on streams, the field \lean{run\_identity};
\item \lean{calls\_node\_le}: the node makes at most $s$ child calls;
\item \lean{Global.pr97} : \lean{BitNetwork}, with \lean{runs\_pr97} and \lean{pr97\_tau}.
\end{itemize}
The node uses $3217908$ zero temporaries, each in the frame its first gate needs, since a zero
stream is valid in every frame.

\section{The machine and the seam}\label{sec:machine}

\lean{M8.BitNetwork} packages a bit network: contract roles and temporaries, the width $m$, the
prime, a control list of edges, XOR gates and resets, the route, and the certificates
\lean{calls\_exact}, \lean{legal}, \lean{legal\_mod}, \lean{xor\_gate}, \lean{run\_identity} and
\lean{branching}. \lean{Runs} adds the shared prime, the coefficient bound and $m \le W$.

The machine \lean{M8.Machine} is the Shared50 recursive machine made generic in the network
\tagL:
\begin{itemize}
\item \lean{Induction.contract} at every depth, and \lean{RootExecution.halts\_power\_bound}: the
  root halts within $C \cdot V \cdot w^{\tau}$ with the array transposed;
\item \lean{BinaryInterchangeRun.runs} and \lean{BinaryInterchangeRun.uniform\_bound}: the
  rectangular interchange in $C \cdot V \cdot \max(1, h, d)^{\tau}$, $\tau$ the network's
  exponent.
\end{itemize}
The arbitrary-width tail uses $43$ modules of the tree unchanged and $76$ ported ones.

The cost chain reaches the interchange through three modules of \lean{Machine}:
\lean{BinaryInterchangeRun}, \lean{BinaryInterchangeBudget} and \lean{BinaryRadixEqualShared}. They are seams that
re-export the chosen supplier, and \lean{Parameters.tau} is the supplier's exponent; Shared50's
own exponent is \lean{Shared50Parameters.tau}. No consumer of the interchange changes. The script
\texttt{scripts/s6/apply\_seam.py} selects \texttt{shared50}, \texttt{m8} (Shared50 as a
network on the generic machine, \lean{Shared50Network}) or \texttt{pr97}; it is idempotent.

\section{The retune}\label{sec:retune}

\tagL{} \lean{Global.Branching97.branching} gives $s'/W < 529^{\tau_{97}}$ for every $s' \le s$
with
\[
  \tau_{97} = 1 - 3734039 \cdot 10^{-12},
\]
within $10^{-12}$ of the threshold, from a $26$-term series for $\log 529$ and rationals. With
$\lambda = 1 - 35 \cdot 10^{-7}$ and $\lambda' = 1 - 33 \cdot 10^{-7}$ the margins are
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\toprule
margin & value & slack over $\kappa$\\
\midrule
layout $\varepsilon c(1-\tau)$ & $1.582591 \cdot 10^{-6}$ & $6.9 \cdot 10^{-15}$\\
layers $\varepsilon(1-\lambda')$ & $1.598438 \cdot 10^{-6}$ & $1.6 \cdot 10^{-8}$\\
CRT $(1-\varepsilon)(1-\tau)$ & $1.925364 \cdot 10^{-6}$ & $3.4 \cdot 10^{-7}$\\
resampling $\tfrac12 - \delta - \varepsilon(1+2^{-33})$ & $7.8125 \cdot 10^{-3}$ &
$7.8 \cdot 10^{-3}$\\
direct $\varepsilon(1-c)$ & $6.0547 \cdot 10^{-2}$ & $6.1 \cdot 10^{-2}$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\begin{theorem}[\lean{MathlibTM2.mulInTime}; \tagL]
One fixed \lean{Turing.FinTM2} machine multiplies in time $O\bigl(n (\log n)^{1-\kappa}\bigr)$
with $\kappa = 111364923 \cdot 2^{-46} \approx 2^{-19.27}$.
\end{theorem}

The F$_p$ transform of~\cite{DFT} inherits the same saving through
\lean{DFT.finalHostMultiplier}.

\section{Next steps}\label{sec:next}

\subsection{Stage 2: the opposite-bank compiler, about $2^{-14.5}$}
The compiler of~\cite{ICE} replaces the $r$ rank-one children of a frame change with one
reversed child $F_{rf}(H, D) = (C_{rf} D, C_{rf} H)$ of rank $r f$ over fields of $f$ atoms, and
stops the recursion at $w = e^{1/1000}$. On plain frames its whole-projector moment gives $a^* =
9.70 \cdot 10^{-5}$, so $\kappa \approx 2^{-14.5}$ \tagC. Done so far \tagL:
\begin{itemize}
\item \lean{exists\_generic\_basis\_of\_idempotent}: one basis with natural entries bounded in
  terms of $m$ and the ranks gives every residual projector nonzero leading minors, by Mathlib's
  combinatorial Nullstellensatz;
\item \lean{revExchange\_generic} and \lean{revExchange\_atoms}: in that basis a projector's
  reversed exchange is lower-triangular passes, shears and the $rf$ interchanges of one child
  $F_{rf}$, and the adapters read only earlier atoms (\lean{lower\_kron\_one}).
\end{itemize}
Remaining: the stopped recurrence $T(n, w) \le A n + B n^{\tau^*} w^{1 - a_{\mathrm{leaf}}}$ from
two moment gaps; the atom adapters at cost $O(Vn)$ per node; the reversed recursive family, which
needs variable-rank block children and nodes at every width; and the outer wrapper $F L F$. The
seam stays, so the switch is again one script run and one retune.

\subsection{Further options}
\begin{itemize}
\item Upstream's design-B frames raise $a^*$ to $1.2402 \cdot 10^{-4}$, about $2^{-14.1}$, with
  about 15 MB of integer frame data.
\item PR589 needs either one window family for all $111$ ports or a two-unit corner repair; the
  latter would give $a_b \le 4.06 \cdot 10^{-4}$, about $2^{-12.4}$.
\item The Gaussian margin limits $\varepsilon < 1/2$. The phase-cell inverse of the NearOne
  plan lifts it toward $2/3$, which scales every margin above by about $1.37$.
\item With the generic machine carrying the theorem, the Shared50 tail and root
  (\lean{Shared50BinaryInterchangeRun} and its dependencies) can retire.
\end{itemize}
